Why some clocks are awkward

Method

Unknowns
One rate per column: a recipe, a generator burning one fuel, or the extraction of one resource. Rates are machine-equivalents at 100% clock; extraction is counted in reference extractors (30/min by default).
Legal clock
A total of x machine-equivalents is buildable with clocks of at most 4 decimals in percent exactly when 106x is an integer. Splitting over machines never helps or hurts: sums of legal clocks are legal, and a legal total always splits. A budget of d decimals means 102+dx is an integer.
Products
Proportional: net outputs equal s·p for one scale s (kept inside its band). Exact: net output equals e. Adjustable: net output anywhere in [min, max]. Every other item nets exactly zero. Power is the one inequality.
Recipe choice
A mixed-integer program minimises Σ extraction / world supply, with at most one recipe per primary product and at most one generator recipe outside the uranium–plutonium–ficsonium chain. It only picks the set; nothing exact depends on its floating-point answer.
The lattice of clocks
With the recipe set fixed, exact elimination writes every rate as an affine function of d free parameters. Requiring every column on its grid cuts that space down to an affine integer lattice, kept in Hermite normal form. Each column then ranges over a progression offset + k·step; the step is the finest move any exact plan allows it.
Rigid groups
Columns whose rates stay in fixed ratio in every exact plan. Pinning one fixes the group. A ratio p/q with a prime other than 2 or 5 in q forces a divisibility; powers of 2 and 5 only cost decimals.
Levers and conflicts
For each exact rate and pin, the page drops that one constraint and reports the values it could take. When no exact plan exists, a deletion filter finds a small set of columns that already conflict, and the balances are reduced to explicit integer relations among them.
Snapping
The concrete plan is the lattice point nearest the previous plan that satisfies every inequality, found by HiGHS over an LLL-reduced basis (the Aardal–Hurkens–Lenstra reformulation). The solver's floats only choose the integer coordinates; the plan is recomputed and every inequality re-checked in exact rational arithmetic.
